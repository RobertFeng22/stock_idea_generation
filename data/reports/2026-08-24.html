1. NET Cloudflare Inc.
4 个来源印证 🔥6 monthshigh
Cloudflare sits at the intersection of three independently convergent theses: (1) AI agent traffic is approaching parity with human traffic and Cloudflare is explicitly named as a default destination for agents selecting hosting solutions; (2) its Workers AI / AI Gateway product is purpose-built for the latency-sensitive, multi-model inference routing use case demanded by security blue teams and live-commerce platforms; (3) the AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) wave rewards infrastructure that AI agents already prefer — and Cloudflare is cited as exactly that. These three vectors are distinct and come from different shows, making this the strongest multi-source convergence in the dataset.
🔍 为何尚未被定价：Cloudflare is covered primarily as a DDoS/CDN/Zero-Trust security vendor. The AI agent traffic tailwind, model-routing infrastructure play, and AEO incumbent advantage are second- and third-order implications that are not broken out in any sell-side model and are not driving current consensus estimates. The 1000x agent-to-human traffic projection (Cloudflare's own data, cited in 20VC) has not been absorbed into valuation frameworks that still anchor on human-traffic CDN economics.

· AI agents are already biased toward Cloudflare when selecting hosting — making it a structural default destination in the agent economy
“We're three years into AI, and we're already at parity in terms of agent traffic to human traffic. I think it was Cloudflare this morning said that in five years from now, we'll have 1,000 times the amount of agent traffic to human traffic... agents are already very biased. They go to Cloudflare and Vercel when they're looking for a hosting solution.” — The Twenty Minute VC — 20VC: Inside Sequoia's Investment Committee
· OpenRouter's model-routing position (acquired by Stripe for ~$7B) is structurally analogous to Cloudflare's AI Gateway product, validating the monetization model

· Security blue teams need multi-model inference flexibility — exactly the routing and fallback capability Cloudflare's AI Gateway provides
“The most important part for blue teams is because of the pace of progress, to have as much flexibility as possible... when Astra gets released by OpenAI, I have an upgrade path... teams have probably two or three options... you can roll with codex or a Claude code and lock yourself into a specific model provider... vendor lock-in is always good [sarcastically].” — The a16z Show — How Do You Defend Against AI That Can Hack?

风险提示：Cloudflare's AI Gateway and Workers AI remain nascent, low-revenue products today; frontier labs could vertically integrate routing natively, and open-source tooling could serve the model-routing use case without a paid intermediary. Also, NET already trades at premium multiples — if the market partially prices in AI tailwinds broadly, the incremental upside from these specific drivers narrows.
正方 🐂：Four independent, informed sources converge on Cloudflare as the structural default destination in the emerging agent economy — a position analogous to AWS for web apps in 2008. Agent traffic scaling 1000x over 5 years means even a small monetization rate per agent call generates a step-change in revenue that no current sell-side model captures. The AI Gateway product is a legitimate OpenRouter analogue that Stripe just validated as worth $7B+ as a business model.
反方 🐻：Cloudflare's AI revenue is effectively zero today and the agent-traffic advantage is based on current AI model defaults that could change with each major model release. The company already trades at 20x+ forward revenue, leaving little margin of safety if the AI product monetization takes longer than 12 months to materialize.
正方反驳 ↩︎：The 'zero revenue today' bear case ignores that the same was true of AWS in 2006 relative to web hosting incumbents. Cloudflare's structural position — being on the token path between agents and the internet — compounds with every new agentic deployment regardless of which model wins. The agent-traffic default bias is driven by technical performance and global PoP density, not model-maker preference, making it sticky across model generations.

The rising-yields tailwind (real assets outperform speculative labs in a tighter capital environment) is a second-order catalyst also not in consensus.

The merchant power price risk in a bust is real but overstated: data center PPAs are long-term, fixed-price contracts that insulate Vistra from spot market volatility.
